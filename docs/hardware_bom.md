Notes
-----
* Buy the fasteners as assortment packs -- the per-piece prices above are what the
  quantities are worth, not what a vendor will sell you a dozen of.
* Every pivot is a **clevis in double shear**: the pin passes cheek-tongue-cheek
  and is retained by an E-clip at each end, so no pin is cantilevered.
* The STS3215 case screws thread into the servo's **own tapped flanges** -- they
  need no insert, but the printed boss must relieve the flanges (54 mm across,
  vs a 45 mm case).
* Verify the servo's flange hole pattern against your actual STS3215 before
  ordering screw lengths; the CAD pattern is indicative.

> **All 12 servos have a modelled housing.** Every actuator has a pocket,
> case screws and a horn interface cut for it in a printed part, and
> `python -m analysis.actuator_fit` confirms each one has the room.
>
> Two of them could not be housed on their own joint and are driven
> through a four-bar from wherever there WAS room, the way the hip already
> was: the **tail** (its pivot is at the rear extremity of the frame) and
> the head's **yaw** (the head is the last link in the chain, so a servo
> inside it can only reach the joint immediately above — the other axis has
> to come from the chest). Head **roll** is not a joint at all: the head
> holds two housings and roll is the one axis a camera can correct in
> software, so it went to electronic stabilisation. The **ears** are rigid
> for the same arithmetic.
